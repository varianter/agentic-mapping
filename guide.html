What did you need to learn? A 15-minute mapping

We don’t bring a list of tools or methods. We want to hear what you had to learn to work well with agents, what you’re still missing, and what took you by surprise. Put together, the answers become a learning path for developers, designers and product people, and a map of the unknown unknowns.

The model: three levels, three questions, three dimensions

The same three questions at each level, asked for the individual, the team and the organization. What goes in the boxes comes from them.

Getting startedThe first weeks of working with agents
Getting goodWorking this way every day, at the quality clients expect
Next levelWhat they are reaching for now
What to learn
Why it mattersWhat goes wrong without it
How you found outWhere the unknown showed up

Working with agents changes things on three axes, and what you need to learn differs on each. Fill in the grid once per dimension.

Individual

How they work on their own: their habits, judgement and craft with agents.

Team

How the team works together: reviews, handovers, shared rules and roles.

Organization

What the organization enables or gets in the way of: access, policy, clients, how work is bought and measured.

Unknown unknowns

The third row and the last block are where these show up: things nobody told them they needed, found by hitting a wall, a bug, a confused client or a colleague who worked differently.

Run of show

  1. 0–2 min

    Context

    “What is your role, and what are you working on right now?”
    “What does really good look like to you, when someone works with agents in your discipline?”

  2. 2–8 min

    Looking back

    “What did you have to learn to get where you are? What came first?”
    For each thing: “What goes wrong if you skip it? How did you find out you needed it?”
    Then widen it: “What did your team have to learn? And the organization around you?”

  3. 8–12 min

    Looking ahead

    “What would the next level look like for you?”
    “What do you think you need to learn to get there? What would the team, or the organization, need to change?”
    “What do you suspect you’re still missing?”

  4. 12–15 min

    Surprises and advice

    “What surprised you most along the way?”
    “Someone in your discipline starts tomorrow. What should they learn first, and what can wait?”
    “Who else should I talk to?”

Don’t lead

Don’t offer examples or name tools. If they stall, ask about one recent piece of work instead.

Their words

Write down what they say, as they say it. Don’t teach or argue; save disagreements for the group session.

Sort afterwards

Categories come from the answers, not from us. Group the notes across conversations once they’re all in.
